# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** openheights

**Short description:** Measure the height of a tower or tree with trigonometry and a phone: shadows, angles, errors and simulation in 3 sessions.

**Full description:** openheights is a beginner-friendly learning repo about one practical question: how tall is that tower or tree? In three short sessions you go from similar triangles and the shadow method, to the angle of elevation and height = distance x tan(angle), then to measuring when the base is out of reach (two angles), to how a half-degree reading error spreads into a height error and where to stand to keep it small. Finally you simulate noisy phone readings, correct for a sloping hill, average many readings with a standard error, compare the one-angle and two-angle methods by Monte Carlo against the exact geometry and the error formula, and see why a reading near 90 degrees blows the error up. Every session follows the same six steps (real-world problem, theory, visual explanation, interactive simulation, worked example, assessment), and has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL. The repo is intentionally incomplete: a Target animation and a roadmap show where the community can take it next, such as phone-sensor calibration, outlier-proof averaging and leaning trees.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
