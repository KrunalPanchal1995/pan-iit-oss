# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** opencrashsim

**Short description:** Learn why crumple zones work: momentum, impulse, energy and a simple crash simulation, in 3 sessions.

**Full description:** opencrashsim is a beginner-friendly learning repo about car crashes seen as a physics problem. In three short sessions you go from the idea that stopping over a longer distance needs a smaller force, to momentum, impulse and work-energy with a design problem (how much crumple distance keeps the deceleration below 20 g from 50 km/h, and what happens at 100 km/h), to a lumped crumple-zone model with a force-displacement curve that you step through time and validate against the exact constant-force answer and the energy balance. You also crash two cars into each other, vary the plateau force and the mass ratio, and make a crumple zone fail by bottoming out. Every session follows the same six steps (real-world problem, theory, visual explanation, interactive simulation, worked example, assessment), and has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL. The models are simplified and educational only: this is not a crashworthiness design tool and makes no injury predictions. The repo is intentionally incomplete: a Target animation and a roadmap show where the community can take it next, such as occupant restraint, staged crumple profiles and off-centre crashes.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
