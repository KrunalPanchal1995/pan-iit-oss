# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** openregularity

**Short description:** What do we know, and not know, about smooth Navier-Stokes solutions? Experiments with Burgers and 2-D flow in 3 sessions.

**Full description:** openregularity is a beginner-friendly learning repo about one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems: do smooth solutions of the 3-D Navier-Stokes equations always exist, or can they develop infinite gradients in finite time (as stated by Fefferman)? That question is OPEN. This repo does not claim, hint at or attempt a proof. It teaches what the problem asks, which results are known (in your own reading you will meet the names Leray, Ladyzhenskaya, Kato, Beale-Kato-Majda, Prodi-Serrin and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg), and what remains unknown. You experiment on simpler models that show the key mechanisms. Session 1 compares a quantity that stays finite with one that blows up in finite time. Session 2 studies the 1-D viscous Burgers equation: steepening into a shock without viscosity, an exact smooth solution with viscosity, the energy equation, and how many Fourier modes are needed. Session 3 solves 2-D Navier-Stokes in vorticity form with a pseudo-spectral method, evaluates the 3-D vortex stretching term on a given field, and shows that a numerical solver can blow up with no real singularity behind it. Each session has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
