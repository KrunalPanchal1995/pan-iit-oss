# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** openclassifier

**Short description:** Learn how classifiers decide and how to tell a good one from a lucky one: boundaries, trees, confusion matrices, ROC, fairness, in 5 sessions.

**Full description:** openclassifier is a beginner-friendly learning repo about classification as a decision problem. In five short sessions you go from a single yes/no rule that sorts fruit and the two kinds of mistake it can make, to decision trees and nearest neighbours written from scratch in NumPy, the confusion matrix and the base-rate trap, and finally to the tools that tell a trustworthy classifier from a lucky one: ROC curves and AUC, bagged trees (a random forest), overfitting, cross-validation, calibration, a fairness comparison across two synthetic groups, and two classic failures (data leakage and class imbalance), then the honest way to tune a forest (choosing the tree depth by cross-validation on the training data only, and seeing what peeking at the test set costs), and last how to give each of two groups its own threshold so that their false-positive rates agree, what that costs when the groups differ, and why the fairness definitions cannot all hold at once. Every session follows the same six steps (real-world problem, theory, visual explanation, interactive simulation, worked example, assessment) and has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL against hand calculations. All data are synthetic and the repo makes no claims about real people. The repo is intentionally incomplete: a Target animation and a roadmap show where the community can take it next.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
