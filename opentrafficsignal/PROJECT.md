# Project form (Hello FOSS)

**Project name:** opentrafficsignal

**Short description:** Learn how traffic signals coordinate traffic: queues, delay and timing, from scratch in 3 sessions.

**Full description:** opentrafficsignal is a beginner-friendly learning repo about one everyday question: how long should each road get green so that people wait less? In three short sessions you go from a four-way junction with two phases and a queue that grows on red and shrinks on green, to the average delay of that queue as a formula of cycle time, green split and flow (Webster's uniform delay), to a seeded random-arrival simulation that compares fixed timing with a simple actuated rule and is checked against the formula, against conservation of cars, and against Webster's optimum cycle. Every session follows the same six steps (real-world problem, theory, visual explanation, interactive simulation, worked example, assessment), and has a lesson script, a Colab notebook, a live slider app and a checkpoint that prints PASS or FAIL. The models are simplified and educational; this is not a traffic engineering tool. The repo is intentionally incomplete: a Target animation and a roadmap show where the community can take it next, such as time-varying demand, smarter adaptive rules, turning phases and green waves.

**Technologies (comma-separated):** Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, JavaScript, LaTeX
