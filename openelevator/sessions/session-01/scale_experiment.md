# Experiment sheet: openelevator, Session 1: a bathroom scale in a lift

Name: ____________________   Date: ____________   Lift / building: ____________________

> **Safety first.** Do this experiment only in lifts you are allowed to use, with permission from whoever is responsible for the building, and preferably with a friend. Never block the doors, never jump or bounce in the car, keep the scale flat in the middle of the floor and step off it before the doors open. If other passengers need the lift, let them use it. This is a simplified educational model, not a test of the lift.

## What you need
- A bathroom scale (a digital one with a large display is easiest to read; a phone camera filming the display helps).
- Optional: a phone app that shows vertical acceleration, to measure a directly.
- This sheet and a pencil.

## The idea
A scale measures the force of the floor on you, the apparent weight N = m (g + a), and shows it as a mass. So the reading is

    reading = m (g + a) / g = m (1 + a / g)        (g = 9.81 m/s^2, a upward positive)

From a reading you can work backwards to the acceleration: a = g (reading / m - 1).

## Steps
1. On solid ground, stand still on the scale and write down your true reading m: ________ kg.
2. In the lift, put the scale in the middle of the floor and stand still on it before the doors close.
3. Ride **up** several floors. Note the reading at rest, the highest reading while the lift starts, the steady reading while it cruises, and the lowest reading while it stops.
4. Ride **down** the same floors and note the same four readings.
5. Repeat each ride at least twice; a real scale needs a moment to settle, so read the value that stays for about a second.
6. Fill in the table, then work out a = g (reading / m - 1) for each line.

## Expected readings (model)
Multiply your true reading m by the factor 1 + a / g. The last two columns are worked out for a 60 kg person (true weight 60 * 9.81 = 588.6 N).

| Acceleration a (m/s^2) | When it happens | Factor 1 + a / g | Floor force for 60 kg (N) | Scale reading for 60 kg (kg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +1.0 | going up and starting, or going down and stopping | 1.102 | 648.6 | 66.1 |
| +0.5 | going up and starting, or going down and stopping (gentle lift) | 1.051 | 618.6 | 63.1 |
| 0 | at rest or cruising at any speed | 1.000 | 588.6 | 60.0 |
| -0.5 | going up and stopping, or going down and starting (gentle lift) | 0.949 | 558.6 | 56.9 |
| -1.0 | going up and stopping, or going down and starting | 0.898 | 528.6 | 53.9 |

Check one line by hand: for a = 0.5 m/s^2, N = 60 * (9.81 + 0.5) = 60 * 10.31 = 618.6 N, and 618.6 / 9.81 = 63.1 kg.
